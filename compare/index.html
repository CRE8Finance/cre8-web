Buying bStock on Binance

Binance offers US stocks to users outside the US, and bStock is the on-chain version of those stocks on BNB Chain, about 105 of them today. Stocks and bStock convert 1:1 on Binance at no fee.

Good at: the deepest liquidity and the widest choice, recurring buys, and bStock can be used as margin collateral.

Trade-offs: you need a verified Binance account, and it isn’t available everywhere (not in the US). You buy one stock at a time, there are no baskets, nothing keeps your weights on target, and the assets sit in your exchange account by default.

Compared with CRE8: If you want one or two popular stocks, or you’re moving a large amount, buying directly on Binance is cheaper. If you want a whole theme in one payment, kept on target and held in your own on-chain account, CRE8 is less work.

xStocks, Ondo and other tokenized stocks

Besides bStock, other issuers tokenize US stocks. Ondo and xStocks are the two biggest. The tokens sit in your own wallet.

Good at: self-custody, and the tokens work across chains and DeFi apps. Ondo also has a few portfolio products on BNB Chain.

Trade-offs: the same stock often comes in several versions and you pick one yourself. On BNB Chain, on-chain pools for Ondo and xStocks tokens are thin. Fake tokens copying real tickers exist too, so you have to check contracts.

Compared with CRE8: CRE8 uses bStock and major crypto only, and an asset goes into a portfolio only after it’s confirmed buyable.

Venzo

An app from City Protocol. On BNB Chain it offers a Mag7 portfolio built from bStock and a Pelosi portfolio built from Ondo tokens (11 stocks, 30 USDT minimum). Each user gets an on-chain smart account, and there’s a 0.1% fee on each buy and sell.

Good at: it’s live on mainnet with real money, backed by well-known investors.

Trade-offs: a small number of portfolios, no yield on idle cash inside the account, and automatic rebalancing and theme portfolios are still on its roadmap. Its main business is yield vaults on other chains.

Compared with CRE8: The setups are similar. CRE8 has more portfolios (21 across people, themes and leverage) and already rebalances automatically. But CRE8 is still on testnet, and Venzo is live.

Opening a US brokerage account

Open an account with a broker, fund it and hold shares in your own name.

Good at: almost every US stock, cash dividends, voting rights, and you own the shares themselves.

Trade-offs: account opening and verification, and only people in some countries can open one. Funding goes through a bank, so USDT has to be cashed out first, and trading follows market hours.

Compared with CRE8: If your money is already in a bank and opening an account is easy where you live, a broker is the most direct route. If your money is in USDT and you’d rather not cash out, on-chain stock tokens skip that step.

CRE8’s own trade-offs

To be straight about it: CRE8 is still on testnet, with no real money. It offers fewer assets than Binance. On-chain markets are shallower than an exchange, so large orders pay more slippage. Stock legs only trade while the US market is open. And bStock is a token, not a share in your name.
