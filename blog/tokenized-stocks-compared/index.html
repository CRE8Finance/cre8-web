Tokenized Stocks Compared: bStock vs xStocks vs Ondo and More

By CRE8 · Nov 23, 2026

Five issuers account for most tokenized US stocks: Binance-affiliated bStock, Kraken's xStocks, Ondo, Robinhood and Dinari. All of them back their tokens with shares held in custody. They differ in legal form, how dividends are paid, which chains they run on, whether you can move tokens to your own wallet, and who is allowed to buy. If your funds are on Binance or BNB Chain, bStock and Ondo are the easiest fit; for the widest catalog, look at Ondo; for Solana and other chains, xStocks; and for US investors, Dinari is currently the only one open to eligible buyers.

What is a tokenized stock, and who issues them?

A tokenized stock is a blockchain token whose price tracks a US stock, backed by shares or ETFs that the issuer holds in custody. What you own is a certificate, note or contract from the issuer, not a share registered in your name.

As of November 23, 2026, rwa.xyz puts the total value of tokenized US stocks at about $3.37 billion across roughly 4.97 million holders. By size: bStock about $1.2 billion, Ondo about $853 million, xStocks about $648 million and Robinhood about $133 million. Dinari isn't in the top 10 (rwa.xyz). rwa.xyz figures don't always match what issuers report themselves, so treat them as a sense of scale.

What do you legally own with each one?

None of the five registers shares in your name. The difference is what kind of claim you get.

bStock is a certificate issued by BTech Holdings under a prospectus approved by Abu Dhabi's ADGM Financial Services Regulatory Authority. It represents an interest in securities the issuer holds, and Binance's own page says "bStocks are not shares" (Binance Learn).

Ondo's stock tokens are structured notes, a type of debt instrument, offered outside the US under Regulation S (Ondo legal docs).

xStocks are issued by Backed Assets in Jersey. Each token is backed 1:1 by the matching share or ETF, and each ticker is held separately (xStocks docs).

Robinhood puts it most bluntly: holders get economic exposure only, with no legal or beneficial rights in the underlying stock. Its original EU version was a derivative contract with Robinhood Europe (Robinhood EU FAQ). Since July 1, 2026, the new version is tokenized debt securities issued by Robinhood Assets (Jersey) (Robinhood newsroom).

Dinari's tokens are backed by the corresponding securities held with a qualified custodian (The Block).

Who holds the shares, and how do you know they exist?

xStocks discloses the most about custody: Alpaca Securities as primary custodian, InCore Bank as secondary, additional Lloyd's coverage of up to $175 million, and a weekly proof of reserves (Kraken FAQ).

bStock and Ondo don't name their custodians but report more often. bStock publishes a daily proof of collateral (Binance Learn). Ondo gets a daily third-party attestation, a separate security agent holds a security interest in the assets, and the tokens are overcollateralized, meaning assets in custody exceed the value of tokens issued (Ondo docs).

We couldn't find official statements naming custodians for Robinhood's new version or for Dinari.

How are dividends paid?

Dinari is the only one that pays cash. It sends dividends in USDC, as long as your wallet is registered with Dinari (Dinari docs).

The others turn dividends into more tokens. bStock deducts 30% US withholding tax and then increases your token count with a multiplier. Ondo reinvests after tax. xStocks reinvests automatically through a multiplier (Binance Learn, Ondo docs, Kraken FAQ). Robinhood's original EU version paid cash dividends; the new version hasn't said.

Can you hold them in your own wallet, and which work on BNB Chain?

bStock, xStocks and Ondo can all be withdrawn to a wallet you control, and all three are on BNB Chain.

bStock exists only on BNB Chain and can go to any BNB Chain-compatible wallet after eligibility and sanctions checks (Binance Learn). rwa.xyz puts bStock at about $1.2 billion on BNB Chain, the largest tokenized stock on that network (rwa.xyz BNB Chain).

Ondo runs on Ethereum, BNB Chain and Solana. On BNB Chain it trades mainly on PancakeSwap (BNB Chain blog).

xStocks covers the most chains and has offered 50+ tickers on BNB Chain since April 30, 2026 (BNB Chain blog).

Robinhood's new version lives on its own Robinhood Chain, held in the non-custodial Robinhood Wallet. Dinari runs on Ethereum, Avalanche, Arbitrum and Base, and every trading wallet must pass KYC; trades from unverified wallets fail (Dinari KYC). Neither is on BNB Chain today.

For a closer look at self-custody on BNB Chain, see Can you hold US stock tokens in your own wallet?

Can you use them as collateral in DeFi?

On BNB Chain, bStock has the clearest DeFi support. Venus added TSLAB, NVDAB and SPCXB to its Core Pool as collateral on June 20, 2026 (CryptoSlate), and Lista DAO added NVDAB, TSLAB and MUB around June 16 (Crypto Briefing).

xStocks lists Kamino and Morpho among places to borrow against its tokens; on BNB Chain, Venus and Flux integrations are described as planned (Kraken FAQ, BNB Chain blog). Ondo lists Morpho and other protocols, and on BNB Chain its main venue is PancakeSwap. Dinari's US clients can't use their tokens in DeFi (Dinari docs).

What does buying NVIDIA with 1,000 USDT look like at each issuer?

Say you hold 1,000 USDT on BNB Chain and want NVIDIA exposure.

bStock: buy on Binance spot from $5, or withdraw to a wallet and swap on PancakeSwap. You could later post it on Venus, where NVDAB has a 60% collateral factor, so a $1,000 position lets you borrow up to $600.

Ondo: buy through PancakeSwap on BNB Chain. If you complete Ondo's KYC, you can later redeem the tokens directly for cash or stablecoins at a 0.05% fee, which is $0.50 on $1,000 (Ondo investing and redeeming).

xStocks: check whether the ticker you want is among the 50+ on BNB Chain. On Kraken, buying with USDG or USD has no fee, while Convert charges 1%, which is $10 on $1,000 (Kraken FAQ).

Robinhood and Dinari: neither is on BNB Chain, so you'd need to move to another chain and wallet, and Dinari requires a KYC'd wallet first.

Dividends land differently too. The same dividend becomes extra tokens with bStock, Ondo and xStocks, and arrives as USDC in your registered wallet with Dinari.

Which issuer fits your situation?

Your funds are on Binance or BNB Chain and you want to borrow against stock tokens on Venus or Lista: bStock is the simplest fit. It's the largest tokenized stock on BNB Chain, and both Venus and Lista accept some bStocks as collateral.

You want the most tickers: Ondo has 450+, Dinari 724 but with wallet KYC, and xStocks 131 on Kraken (Ondo docs, KuCoin News, Kraken FAQ).

You want the option to redeem directly with the issuer: Ondo documents this most clearly, with a $1 minimum and 0.05% fee once you pass KYC.

Your assets are on Solana or another chain: xStocks covers 10 chains (Finance Magnates).

You want cash dividends: Dinari pays in USDC.

You're in the US: bStock, xStocks, Ondo and Robinhood all exclude US persons. Dinari opened to eligible US investors on August 4, 2026 (The Block).

You want full shareholder rights: none of the five offer that; buy real shares through a broker. Our overview, How to buy US stocks with USDT, compares those routes.

What are the risks?

You don't own a share. With every issuer, your rights depend on the issuer and custodian, and an issuer failure means an insolvency process. An executive director of the European Securities and Markets Authority has warned that tokenized stocks usually carry no shareholder rights and that retail buyers may misunderstand them (CryptoSlate).

Transfers can be restricted. Dinari's docs say flagged wallets may be blocked from transfers and trading, and exchanges reserve the right to restrict accounts (Dinari docs, Binance Learn).

Fake tokens. Copycat tokens with the same ticker appear on DEXs; only trust the contract address the issuer publishes. The NYSE and the World Federation of Exchanges have both warned about fake tokenized stocks (crypto.news).

Prices drift when the market is closed. Tokens keep trading after the US close while issuers can't hedge, so prices can open far from the next session. xStocks minting and redemption run 24/5 only, so selling on a weekend depends on finding a buyer (xStocks docs).

Regional limits. Most issuers don't publish a full list of restricted countries, so check each platform's terms before you buy.

FAQ

Can I swap one issuer's token for another's directly? No. They are different tokens from different issuers. Sell to USDT first, then buy the other.

Which issuer is the safest? There is no single answer. Custody, proof frequency and legal form all differ; the table shows what each issuer discloses, and the outcome ultimately depends on the issuer and custodian.

How current is this table? These products change quickly. Everything here was checked on November 23, 2026. When an issuer changes its terms, we update this article and its "updated" date.

Can mainland China users buy these? None of the five issuers says publicly whether mainland China is restricted. Check the platform's regional terms first.
