# How to Buy US Stocks With USDT: 5 Ways and Who Each Suits

By CRE8 · November 23, 2026 · 8 min read

If you hold USDT and want US stocks, there are five main routes: buy stock tokens on an exchange, buy stock tokens on-chain yourself, buy real shares through a crypto platform, open an account with an international broker, or use CRE8 to buy a whole basket that sits in your own account. If you want real shares with full shareholder rights, a broker is the answer. If you want to stay in USDT and never touch fiat, stock tokens are. Below, each route is broken down by what you actually get, where your assets sit, and who it fits.

## What's the difference between a stock token and a real share?

A stock token is a token on a blockchain that tracks the price of a US stock, backed 1:1 by shares or ETFs the issuer holds with a custodian. What you own is the issuer's instrument, not the share itself. Binance states plainly that "bStocks are not shares," and Ondo's stock tokens are legally notes ([Binance Learn](https://www.binance.com/en/learn/bstocks), [Ondo docs](https://docs.ondo.finance/ondo-stocks/legal-and-regulatory.md)).

A real share is registered with a broker in your name. It carries full dividend and shareholder rights, but you need to open an account and fund it, and most brokers won't take USDT directly.

As of November 23, 2026, tokenized US stocks across all chains were worth about $3.37 billion, held by about 4.97 million holders. The three largest issuers were Binance's bStock (about $1.2 billion), Ondo (about $852.6 million) and xStocks (about $648.1 million) ([rwa.xyz](https://app.rwa.xyz/stocks)).

## Route 1: How do you buy stock tokens on an exchange?

You sign up for an exchange and place an order with USDT, the same way you'd buy a coin. Binance sells its own bStock with a $5 minimum and 24/7 spot trading. Kraken, Bybit and others sell xStocks; on Kraken you can buy fractions from $1 ([Binance Learn](https://www.binance.com/en/learn/bstocks), [Kraken FAQ](https://support.kraken.com/articles/xstocks-faq)).

Dividends don't arrive as cash. They're turned into more tokens. With bStock, a 30% US withholding tax comes off first.

Best for: people who want a few individual stocks and are comfortable keeping funds on an exchange.

Keep in mind: you buy one stock per order and work out the weights yourself, and your assets sit in your exchange account by default.

## Route 2: How do you buy stock tokens on-chain yourself?

You move USDT to your own wallet and swap it for stock tokens on a decentralized exchange such as PancakeSwap. The tokens stay in your hands the whole time. On BNB Chain, the main issuers are bStock and Ondo, and xStocks has listed 50+ tokens there too ([BNB Chain blog](https://www.bnbchain.org/en/blog/xstocks-is-now-live-on-bnb-chain-with-50-tokenized-equities-with-100-more-coming-soon)). Once you hold them, you can post them as collateral on lending protocols like Venus and Lista.

Best for: people who know DeFi, want self-custody and don't mind doing every step themselves.

Keep in mind: fake tokens with the same ticker are common on-chain, so check the contract address against the one the issuer publishes ([DEXTools](https://www.dextools.io/news/xstocks-on-bnb-chain-the-reality-of-245-trading)). If you borrow against your tokens, watching the liquidation risk is on you.

## Route 3: How do you buy real shares on a crypto platform?

Starting in 2026, several exchanges launched real-stock services backed by licensed US brokers, so what you buy is an actual share, not a token. Binance Stocks offers 7,000+ US stocks and ETFs and converts USDT to USDC when you place an order. MEXC's RealStocks takes USDT directly ([Binance Learn](https://www.binance.com/en/learn/binance-stocks), [MEXC FAQ](https://www.mexc.com/support/article/realstocks-trading-faq-382924861612411904)).

Best for: people who want to pay with USDT but want real shares and a wide selection.

Keep in mind: the shares are executed and held by the platform's partner US broker (Alpaca, in Binance Stocks' case) and can't be moved to your own wallet. MEXC's FAQ states that SIPC protection (the US securities investor protection scheme) applies at the omnibus account level, not to each user individually.

## Route 4: How do you use an international broker?

You open an account with a broker such as Interactive Brokers (IBKR), Futu, Tiger or Firstrade, fund it, and buy shares. Most brokers need you to convert USDT to fiat first. IBKR accepts stablecoin deposits, but only USDC, PYUSD and RLUSD, not USDT, with a 0.30% conversion fee ([IBKR FAQ](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/support/faq/content/deposit-via-stablecoin-1331500535)).

Best for: people who can open and fund a brokerage account and want real shares with full shareholder rights.

Keep in mind: this route is largely closed to mainland China residents. Since May 22, 2026, offshore brokers can no longer accept new purchases or deposits from existing mainland clients, only sales and transfers out. Futu and Tiger applied this from June 12 ([CSRC](https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c100028/c7634326/content.shtml)).

## Do US stock perpetual futures count?

No, they don't give you any stock. A perpetual future is a bet on the price going up or down. There are no dividends, you trade with leverage, and a sharp move can liquidate your position. Perps suit short-term trading, not long-term holding.

## What are the risks?

Stock tokens are not shares. Your rights depend on the issuer and the custodian, and if the issuer fails, you're in a bankruptcy process.

Prices drift when the market is closed. Stock tokens keep trading after the US close, but with fewer market makers, so the price can end up far from the next opening price ([OKX Help Center](https://www.okx.com/help/unified-tokenized-stocks)).

Regional restrictions apply. No stock token is offered to US persons, xStocks also excludes the UK, Canada and Australia, and mainland China residents can only sell at offshore brokers.

Prices can fall. Whichever route you pick, you're buying assets that go down as well as up.

## FAQ

**Which route is cheapest?**
It depends on the amount and how often you buy. Fees vary across exchanges and brokers, and large orders also depend on spreads. This article doesn't compare fees, so check each platform's site before you order.

**Do stock tokens pay dividends?**
Yes, but usually not in cash. Dividends are converted into more tokens, and some issuers deduct US withholding tax first.

**I'm in mainland China. Which routes still work?**
Offshore brokers no longer accept new purchases. Stock token issuers haven't published whether mainland users are restricted, so check each platform's regional terms first.
